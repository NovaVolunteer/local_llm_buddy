Overview

Meeting Title: Zero Day Form Extraction Meeting Attendees began by discussing logistics and confirming attendance, with some participants joining late. The main topic centered on approaches to extracting structured data from previously unseen ("zero day") forms, particularly in the medical domain. The team reviewed their current workflow, which involves using a Vision Language Model (VLM) and Amazon Textract to extract key-value pairs from forms, then mapping these to the OMOP (Observational Medical Outcomes Partnership) standard for medical data. They noted that this process works best when the form structure is known, but is less effective for truly novel or unstructured documents. A key insight from a recent conversation with a colleague (Dave) was shared: rather than focusing on extracting key-value pairs from unknown forms, it may be more effective to simply extract all text and use NLP to identify OMOP concepts (such as blood pressure, medications, injuries, demographics) directly from the text, regardless of its structure. This approach would reduce the risk of "hallucinating" data and focus on high-confidence extraction of known medical concepts, even from unstructured notes or handwritten documents. The group discussed the technical implications of this paradigm shift. They considered training a model specifically to recognize a defined set of OMOP concepts (about 300), which would make the extraction process more focused and testable. They also discussed the potential to use tools like AWS Comprehend Medical for concept extraction, but noted its limitations (e.g., sensitivity to typos, lack of tunability) compared to large language models (LLMs), which could be fine-tuned for this task and potentially run locally for cost efficiency. The conversation then shifted to the value of confidence scores in extraction, the possibility of combining positional/contextual information with NLP outputs, and the broader applicability of this approach to other domains (e.g., financial forms for DFAS). The team brainstormed about building a system that could leverage both model confidence and learned patterns from form layouts to improve extraction accuracy. In the latter part of the meeting, the discussion moved to knowledge management and long-term memory for project documentation. Participants shared experiences with tools like Otter for transcription, Claude and OpenAI's "Dreamer" for persistent project memory, and the challenges of storing, retrieving, and querying project knowledge. They debated the merits of different storage architectures (file trees with YAML/markdown, vector databases, triple stores) and the importance of making project knowledge accessible for both real-time and long-term use, including cross-project knowledge sharing and reporting. The meeting concluded with action items to formalize the new zero day extraction approach for client presentation, and a plan to reconvene the following week to review findings and further develop the proposed workflow. There was also interest in documenting a sample architecture for integrating AI-powered knowledge management into their project ecosystem, particularly leveraging Microsoft and OpenAI tools.
