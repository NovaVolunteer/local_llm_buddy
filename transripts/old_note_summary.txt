Meeting Title: Zero Day Form Extraction Meeting Attendees began by discussing logistics and confirming attendance, with some participants joining late. The team reviewed their current workflow, which involves using a Vision Language Model (VLM) and Amazon Textract to extract key-value pairs from forms, then mapping these to the OMOP (Observational Medical Outcomes Partnership) standard for medical data. They noted that this process works best when the form structure is known, but is less effective for truly novel or unstructured documents. A key insight from a recent conversation with a colleague (Dave) was shared: rather than focusing on extracting key-value pairs from unknown forms, it may be more effective to simply extract all text and use NLP to identify OMOP concepts (such as blood pressure, medications, injuries, demographics) directly from the text, regardless of its structure. This approach would reduce the risk of "hallucinating" data and focus on high-confidence extraction of known medical concepts, even from unstructured notes or handwritten documents. The group discussed the technical implications of this paradigm shift. They considered training a model specifically to recognize a defined set of OMOP concepts (about 300), which would make the extraction process more focused and testable. They also discussed the potential to use tools like AWS Comprehend Medical for concept extraction, but noted its limitations (e.g., sensitivity to typos, lack of tunability) compared to large language models (LLMs), which could be fine-tuned for this task and potentially run locally for cost efficiency. The conversation then shifted to the value of confidence scores in extraction, the possibility of combining positional/contextual information with NLP outputs, and the broader applicability of this approach to other domains (e.g., financial forms for DFAS). The team brainstormed about building a system that could leverage both model confidence and learned patterns from form layouts to improve extraction accuracy. AI models being developed and used as a discrete component of the software
AI models that are being leveraged to develop the software being proposed. 
Or both

A high level framework to assess potential risk might look like below:
Leveraging API calls with limited finetuning or data augmentation - bad
There’s some evidence to suggest that when new models get introduced the previous versions begin to degrade, so over reliance on say GPT 4o is problematic. 
Zero shot being used for specific knowledge dependent task - bad
Larger models fine tuned but being asked to solve multiple tasks across the development pipeline or solution - bad 
Fine tuned large or medium size models for specific tasks - better
No process for data augmentation (RAG) - probably not ideal
Ideally highly tuned offline small or medium size models that use data augmentation methods - good
